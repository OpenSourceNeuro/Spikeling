## OSC-01 — Explain a spike without a black box { #osc-01 }

| **Concept / theme**        | Visitors manipulate a visible control, predict the response and explain a spike using an age-appropriate model.                      |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Audience / context**     | Public and school outreach. Formal practical, workshop or modular course. Prior modules: None.                                       |
| **Logistics**              | 30 min; Flexible; boards: 1–3; software: GUI optional; prepared dataset: No. Equipment: Standard USB cable; worksheet/protocol card. |
| **Mode / stages**          | Outreach demonstration. Stages: 1–3, 10–12.                                                                                          |
| **Spikeling relationship** | 1 — Direct physical implementation; 2 — Direct board + GUI; 7 — Conceptual analogy                                                   |

**Learning outcomes.**

- Identify and predict the principal behaviour described in visitors manipulate a visible control, predict the response and explain a spike using an age-appropriate model.

- Configure or document run a short stimulus-strength and threshold demonstration and record control setting, Vm display, LED/sound event.

- Measure, calculate or compare run a short stimulus-strength and threshold demonstration using an explicit operational rule.

- Interpret the result and state why avoid channel/receptor language and avoid implying that electronic sound/light is a literal neuron

**Roadmap.**

> 1\. Initial prediction or classification.
>
> 2\. Configure the board, GUI, simulation or dataset and record metadata.
>
> 3\. Run a short stimulus-strength and threshold demonstration.
>
> 4\. Acquire or inspect control setting, Vm display, LED/sound event.
>
> 5\. Produce one-sentence explanation plus limitation phrase.
>
> 6\. Compare conditions or models and justify the chosen measurement.
>
> 7\. Answer a limitation question: Avoid channel/receptor language and avoid implying that electronic sound/light is a literal neuron.

**Inputs → outputs.** Students receive a configuration/protocol prompt, variable definitions and any required starter data. They manipulate run a short stimulus-strength and threshold demonstration. The reusable output is one-sentence explanation plus limitation phrase.

**Sources / connections / priority.** Sources: \[T3 Chs.2–5; G1; O7 Communication guidance\]. Natural follow-ons: OSC-02, FND-01. Development priority: Outreach module. Boundary: Avoid channel/receptor language and avoid implying that electronic sound/light is a literal neuron.

## OSC-02 — Facilitate challenge cards and teacher-LED inquiry { #osc-02 }

| **Concept / theme**        | Educators use modular challenge cards to support prediction, manipulation, evidence and explanation at different levels.             |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Audience / context**     | Teacher training and outreach staff. Formal practical, workshop or modular course. Prior modules: OSC-01 or FND-01.                  |
| **Logistics**              | 60 min; Flexible; boards: 1–3; software: GUI optional; prepared dataset: No. Equipment: Standard USB cable; worksheet/protocol card. |
| **Mode / stages**          | Educator workshop. Stages: 1–3, 10–12.                                                                                               |
| **Spikeling relationship** | 1 — Direct physical implementation; 4 — Multi-board implementation; 7 — Conceptual analogy                                           |

**Learning outcomes.**

- Identify and predict the principal behaviour described in educators use modular challenge cards to support prediction, manipulation, evidence and explanation at different levels.

- Configure or document select and adapt one challenge from threshold, inhibition, light response or paired-board logic and record learner predictions, observations and explanations.

- Measure, calculate or compare select and adapt one challenge from threshold, inhibition, light response or paired-board logic using an explicit operational rule.

- Interpret the result and state why challenge language must preserve the distinction between direct behaviour and analogy

**Roadmap.**

> 1\. Initial prediction or classification.
>
> 2\. Configure the board, GUI, simulation or dataset and record metadata.
>
> 3\. Select and adapt one challenge from threshold, inhibition, light response or paired-board logic.
>
> 4\. Acquire or inspect learner predictions, observations and explanations.
>
> 5\. Produce adapted challenge card and facilitation plan.
>
> 6\. Compare conditions or models and justify the chosen measurement.
>
> 7\. Answer a limitation question: Challenge language must preserve the distinction between direct behaviour and analogy.

**Inputs → outputs.** Students receive a configuration/protocol prompt, variable definitions and any required starter data. They manipulate select and adapt one challenge from threshold, inhibition, light response or paired-board logic. The reusable output is adapted challenge card and facilitation plan.

**Sources / connections / priority.** Sources: \[T3 Chs.2–5; G1; O7 Communication guidance\]. Natural follow-ons: MET-01, EPI-04. Development priority: Outreach module. Boundary: Challenge language must preserve the distinction between direct behaviour and analogy.
